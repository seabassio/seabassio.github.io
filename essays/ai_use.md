## What role does AI have in education?

The main role that AI is going to play in education is pretty obscure right now. Whilst an optimistic outlook would say that it is likely that it will be used as a more high-tech Google search, a more critical look would bring up the fact that a lot of students are using AI to complete the entire assignment wihtout human input. When using AI in this way, it is obvious how much it hinders the education of the student using it, as they will display knowledge of the subject without ever having to actually learn the subject.

These concerns are my main issue with AI. Whilst it is impossible to argue with the benefits of getting work done faster, it is also worrying to think about a future where we don't know how to do anything without an internet connection. Technology is already in a state where it is so locked down that it is impossible to get most of it repaired via a third party.

## My experience with AI in my introduction Software Engineering class.

For most of this entire semester, I have refrained from using almost any AI to complete my assignments.

Experience WODs - I did not use AI becaue I wanted to find out how to solve the issue myself.

In class practice WODs - I did not use AI because they were only practice, I did not need a working output in order to get full credit.

In class WODs - I did not use AI because I did not know how to use the output that it gave me in my own solutions. 

Essays - I did not use AI because I always found expressing my thoughts and opinions interesting.

Final project - I used ChatGPT to try and help me solve problems with Prisma, but the output did not work. I ended up looking through the documentation and fixing the problem that way.

Learning concepts - I used the Google AI Overview when learning about new concepts because it is convenient, however most of the time it was not correct.

Answering a question - I would not use AI when answering a question because that would remove the entire reason of why I chose to answer a question, which is to apply my own experience.

Asking a smart question - I like to keep questions concise, and AI usually rambles on and adds too man fluff words for me to use it whilst asking a question.

Coding example - This is actually the best use-case I've seen for AI, since it takes code that has alreay been written and restructures it into a way that anybody can understand and learn from.

Explaining code - I did not use AI to explain code for me, I usually just looked up the syntax myself.

Writing code - I did not use AI to write my code because I do not trust it to give an output that fits with all of the systems and edge-cases that I need to work with.

Documenting code - I can see this being useful in some ways, as it will cut down on time that could be spent doing something else, but I did not use it.

Quality assurance - Figuring out specifc errors and why they're happening is one of the best time-cuts that AI can offer, I used Google's AI for this one.

## How did AI impact my learning and understanding of the concepts?

Because I personally did not use much AI in my assignments, I do not thin that my ability to comprehend the subjects in this class were much too impacted. However, as I undestand it, this class was actually modified to accomodate for the use of AI, which means that it became especially challenging for someone who did not want to use it. I learned very quickly that if I wanted to keep up with the course material, I had to start using AI in some regards. I feel that the accomodation was unfair to a certain extent, but I understand why it was put in place. For one, I wish it was made clearer that this class was made with AI in mind.

## Practical Applications of AI

One key area that I see AI having a huge stake in is data analysis, especially in the medical field. For example, AI could be used to figure out how to best deal with malignant tumors, because it could read through a blood sample blazingly fast and come up with a course of action in minutes. This kind of speed could save a person's life, which is where I find AI excels. Another practical solution for AI is translation, as AI can pick up on more than just plain words spoken- such as tone and dialect- to come up with a much more accurate translation than algorithms gave alone.

## Challenges of AI

The main limitation of AI in it's current state is that it is unreliable and/or expensive for students. If you are using the basic free AI such as ChatGPT or base Google Gemini, you are recieving a much more limited and underpowered version of their paid AI. However, if you were to go through the paid route of AI, it can cost up an arm and a leg for the paid versions of these models, such as the one that Anthropic provides. If the price of these more reliable AI cannot come down, or if these AI do not become open source in the future, I believe that it should not be too heavily integrated into our curriculum.

## Traditional teaching vs. AI teaching

I believe that no matter if AI is integrated into the curriculum or not, the best teaching will always come out of the most passionate teachers. Good teachers understand how students retain information, so they can decide if AI should be an aspect that they should take into account. In the case of a less experienced teacher, however, I can see AI being used in a way such that it makes the course material more digestible.

## AI going into the future

Going forward, I can not see AI being "uninvented". It is now a key thing to consider in the world of software engineering, as the speed of output is just as important as the quality of the output. However, because AI is so popular right now, and people need jobs, I don't see it being as "necessary" to learning as it is now. Once this technology becomes more of a supplementary tool instead of a generation tool, then AI will have a better place in our school curriculum, just like calculators and Google do now.

## Overall...

I believe AI does have it's place in society. While I did not use it during this specific class semester too much, I have realized the importance of AI, and will consider my options going into the future. I believe that if I still keep a curious mind, I will be able to integrate AI into my workflow without stunting my ability to take in information. When I pass on my experience to the next generation of software engineers in the future, I will let them know much of the same thing. Speed in a fast-paced work environment is just as important of a variable as quality is, and neither should be compromised.
